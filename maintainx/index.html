How ChatGPT describes MaintainX: a 30-prompt baseline

Shambhavi Singh · November 3, 2026 · Independent research; not affiliated with MaintainX

ChatGPT knows MaintainX well: it named MaintainX in 24 of 30 buyer questions and recommended it in 14. But MaintainX fades as questions get specific. On industry, feature and problem questions, ChatGPT often cites competitors' pages, even where MaintainX has already published a page on the same topic.

The content gap is not the problem. The question is why these pages are not being picked as sources, which points to structure, freshness and markup (see Fixes 2 and 3).

Finding 2: ChatGPT does not know what MaintainX's features can do

On the three feature questions, MaintainX was named once. The one exception shows what works.

Condition-based maintenance and IoT: MaintainX not mentioned

ChatGPT named six vendors and praised Limble because it “integrates with platforms such as Augury and AssetWatch.” MaintainX has live integration pages for both (/integrations/augury, /integrations/assetwatch) and a /use-cases/condition-based-maintenance page. Limble's own site was the most-cited source. The same gap appears in the Limble vs. MaintainX comparison, which scores MaintainX lower on industrial integrations.

Parts inventory and purchase orders: MaintainX not mentioned

ChatGPT named five vendors, including MaintainNow and MaintainOS. MaintainX's /use-cases/parts-inventory-management page says it lets teams “generate purchase orders automatically” and manage “inventory, vendors, and purchase orders in the same system.”

AI-written work orders: the feature is invisible

Asked how teams can use AI to write procedures and work orders, ChatGPT recommended building your own AI assistant and named MaintainX only as a system to connect it to. MaintainX's own llms.txt describes CoPilot as “an AI assistant that allows users to generate work orders, create procedures, and access equipment manuals using natural language prompts.”

The exception: offline mobile use

For “CMMS that works offline on a phone,” ChatGPT recommended MaintainX as a “strong offline candidate” and cited a MaintainX Help Center article. Fiix, MaintainX and Limble were each cited through their help-center documentation. Plain, task-level documentation is what earned the citation.

Finding 3: ownership is right only where Autodesk's pages are cited

Asked directly, ChatGPT correctly says Autodesk completed its acquisition of MaintainX on Aug. 3, 2026, citing Autodesk's announcement. But in the enterprise-fit answer, where a buyer weighs the long-term roadmap, it still describes the deal as pending:

“Autodesk announced a proposed $3.6 billion acquisition of MaintainX in May 2026, with closing subject to regulatory and customary conditions at the time of the announcement.”

MaintainX's own machine-readable files have not caught up: its llms.txt says Type: Private and still lists “Bard” among AI assistants, and the homepage Organization schema (@id …/#organization) has no parentOrganization. Pricing, by contrast, was correct in every answer that gave it.

Three fixes

1. Update the entity facts

In llms.txt:

Type: Subsidiary of Autodesk, Inc. (part of Autodesk Operations Solutions since August 2026)
Parent company: Autodesk, Inc. (https://www.autodesk.com/)
...intended for AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini...

In the homepage Organization node, one added property:

"parentOrganization": {
  "@type": "Organization",
  "name": "Autodesk, Inc.",
  "url": "https://www.autodesk.com/"
}

2. Give feature pages the shape that earned the offline citation

For condition-based maintenance, the Augury and AssetWatch integrations, parts and purchasing, and CoPilot: add short, self-contained question-and-answer blocks (“Does MaintainX integrate with Augury?”), a visible “last updated” date with matching dateModified, and SoftwareApplication markup. The six comparison pages already use question headings but carry no structured data and no update date. (Google limits FAQ rich results to a few site types, so the value here is machine readability and freshness, not a search feature.)

3. Refresh the three uncited buyer's guides, then re-test

Hotels, restaurants, and food and beverage: date them, open each with a one-paragraph direct answer, and add a comparison table that names MaintainX's fit plainly. Then re-run the same prompts after 30 days and compare named, recommended and cited counts against this baseline.

Method and limits

  • 30 prompts in six groups (category, industry, comparison, problem, feature, MaintainX itself), each run once in a fresh chat on ChatGPT with web search on, Oct. 31 to Nov. 1, 2026, on a new account.
  • Memory was on for the first pass. The eight problem and feature prompts that don't name MaintainX were re-run in temporary chats with memory off; those results are the ones reported.
  • “Named” means MaintainX appears anywhere in the answer. “Recommended” means the answer suggests it for the use case or puts it on its final shortlist. “Cited” means a getmaintainx.com page appears as a source, not just an “official website” link.
  • Every claim about MaintainX's site was checked against getmaintainx.com on Nov. 1 to 3, 2026. Answers vary between runs, so treat single prompts as examples and the groups as the pattern.
